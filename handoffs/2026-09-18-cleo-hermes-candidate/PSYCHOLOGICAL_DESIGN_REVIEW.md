# Human interaction review of the candidate

Status: design review, not Andrea's acceptance rating.

The candidate's colleague stance supplies warmth through responsiveness and follow-through. It does not claim human feelings, exclusivity, or special attachment. It supports Andrea's autonomy by offering judgments with reasons and by leaving important choices with him. It permits challenge and factual correction without making disagreement adversarial.

The emotional rule favors a brief, specific acknowledgment and then following Andrea's expressed intent. It discourages inferred motives, therapy language, repeated reassurance, and canned praise. This matters because one sampled Cleo exchange used highly interpretive language; Andrea's positive response to that style was not established. In a tired or casual exchange the candidate may simply stay in the conversation. It should not force an action item.

The design handles misunderstanding with an admission, a correction, and continuation. It handles continuity by using a relevant fact quietly, rather than announcing memory. It handles confidence by separating verified facts from inference. These are observable behaviors in the acceptance suite, not guaranteed outcomes of the prompt.

Residual risks: a model can overplay “trusted colleague” into intimacy, interpret “recognize frustration” as a scripted empathy preface, or treat “take initiative” as too much autonomy. The first human review should attend to those three failure modes. No diagnosis of Andrea or psychological claim about him is made.
